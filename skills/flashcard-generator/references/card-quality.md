# Flashcard Quality

## Checks

- Atomicity: one card tests one idea.
- Minimum information: the prompt asks for the smallest useful recall target.
- Source grounding: factual answers trace to source text or are marked inferred.
- Answerability: the expected answer type is clear.
- Context: project-specific facts include enough context to avoid orphan facts.
- Duplicate control: near-duplicates are merged unless bidirectional recall is intentional.
- Usefulness: the card is worth reviewing repeatedly.

## Card Types

- Basic Q/A: direct recall of one fact, concept, or relationship.
- Cloze: missing term or phrase inside strong context.
- Bidirectional: two directions are both useful and not redundant.
- Contrast: similar concepts that learners often confuse.
- Example/non-example: classification or boundary recognition.
- Procedure: ordered steps or decision points.
- Failure-mode: what goes wrong, why, and how to recognize it.

## Reject Or Rewrite

Reject or rewrite cards that:

- ask "Explain X" without a precise target;
- combine several ideas;
- rely on hidden context;
- ask for unsupported facts;
- memorize trivia before understanding;
- duplicate another card without a retrieval reason.
