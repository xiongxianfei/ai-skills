# Question Quality

## Checks

- Objective alignment: every item maps to a learning objective.
- Source grounding: factual questions and answers trace to the source or are marked inferred.
- Answerability: the learner can answer from supplied material or clearly marked context.
- Ambiguity: no multiple defensible answers unless explicitly designed.
- Difficulty: effortful but fair for the stated audience.
- Feedback: explains why the answer is right or wrong.
- Redundancy: items test distinct evidence or objectives.

## Useful Item Types

- Recall: tests an important fact or term.
- Comprehension: tests meaning or interpretation.
- Contrast: separates similar concepts.
- Application: uses knowledge in a new but source-supported situation.
- Prediction: asks what should happen next.
- Diagnosis: identifies failure cause or misconception.
- Transfer: applies a principle across contexts.
- Tradeoff judgment: compares reasonable choices with evidence.

## MCQ Rules

- The stem should be clear before reading the options.
- Distractors should be plausible but clearly wrong.
- Avoid trick wording and grammatical clues.
- Avoid "all of the above" and "none of the above" by default.
- Explain each option when feedback is requested or included by default.

## Reject Or Rewrite

Reject or rewrite questions that:

- rely on hidden context;
- ask unsupported advanced facts;
- have more than one best answer unintentionally;
- use shallow recall when the goal is diagnostic transfer;
- lead the learner to the answer through wording clues;
- repeat the same objective without adding diagnostic value.
